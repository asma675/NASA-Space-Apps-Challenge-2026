# BLINDSPOT — World Observatory / 3-minute judge script (2026 NASA world data)

**Audience:** Maya, a global wildfire preparedness analyst trying to make sense of satellite burning activity, not an emergency dispatcher.  
**Problem:** Two NASA sensors can disagree about where and when activity is detected. Adding raw hotspot counts from MODIS and VIIRS would be scientifically misleading.  
**Solution:** A shared 10-km/day comparison, an interactive reveal, a date-and-region activity calendar, source quality flags and a decision-ready briefing.

**00:00–00:30 | Enter the observatory**
"Imagine Maya is reviewing the world's satellite fire history. She has observations from two NASA instruments, MODIS and VIIRS. But the same month can look very different depending on the satellite. When they disagree, what should a preparedness analyst conclude?"

**00:30–01:00 | World proof**
"These aren't generated dots. NASA FIRMS sent us 20.66 million worldwide hotspot rows from November 2025 through November 2026. We kept the original files and indexed 19.57 million observations into a common 10-kilometer, UTC-day comparison. Some standard rows were excluded using NASA's source-type classification; recent rows without that classification are clearly marked provisional."

**01:00–01:40 | The reveal — your wow moment**
"Choose World, June 2026—a month for which both instruments have standard archive records. Drag the map divider. On one side: MODIS. On the other: VIIRS. Now click Reveal Blindspots. We separate the cells/days observed only by MODIS, only by VIIRS, and by both. This is *where the evidence differs*, not a claim that either satellite missed a verified fire."

**01:40–02:15 | From heat to calendar**
"Change the region to Australia or the Amazon. The same source files are filtered to that geography. Pick a month and read the hotspot activity calendar. We never turn a missing satellite window into a zero or invent a multi-decade baseline from one worldwide year."

**02:15–02:45 | What Maya can actually do**
"Now compare with the independent Alberta 2023 case study, where complete original detections are preserved. A readiness brief records what the satellites observed, where they differ, and what still needs verification from fire agencies and local experts."

**02:45–03:00 | Closing**
"BLINDSPOT isn't another wildfire alert map. It shows **what the satellites actually observed, where their stories diverge, and what analysts can responsibly do next.** One planet. Two satellite stories. A clearer decision."

## Formal Space Apps submission warning
The official required project demo is **30 seconds maximum** or a **maximum seven-slide deck**. This three-minute script is for local live judging only if your Local Lead allows it. Any linked demo and app must be publicly accessible and cite NASA, all mapping/agency data, any reused open-source components, and AI assistance as appropriate.

## Scope limitations (say aloud if asked)
- The worldwide period is 2025-11–2026-11. The older 2023 dataset is Alberta only. Do NOT claim a 20-year global calendar or validated long-term anomaly.
- 10-km global Equal Earth grid/day overlap is spatial/temporal alignment, not calibrated sensor detection probability.
- Hotspots are not individual confirmed wildfires. NRT observations are preliminary, and neither forecasted ignition nor verified fire-response timing is measured here.
